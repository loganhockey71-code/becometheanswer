More than you can see. When an AI names a competitor instead of you, no alarm goes off; the lost sale never reaches your analytics. But the revenue is real. AI invisibility is a silent, compounding cost: the category questions you would have won, answered by someone else.

The hardest cost to act on is the one you cannot see, and AI invisibility is exactly that. When a buyer asks an assistant what to buy and it names three brands that are not you, nothing shows up in your dashboard. There is no bounce, no lost click, no failed conversion to investigate. The sale simply happened somewhere you were never present. The zeros accumulate silently while your reports still look healthy, which is why most brands are paying this cost without knowing the number.

But invisible is not the same as free. Every category question where the machine recommends a rival and omits you is a sale you did not know you were in the running for. Multiply that by the questions in your category, every day, and the silent cost becomes a real one.

You can size it. The book uses a simple model, Answer Economy Revenue, or AER, to turn invisibility into a figure a CFO recognises:

AER equals category AI query volume multiplied by your Share of Answer, multiplied by AI click-through rate, multiplied by conversion rate, multiplied by average customer value.

Feed in the monthly questions your category generates, how often AI currently names you, and your own conversion economics, and you get an annual revenue-at-stake number. In the book's worked example, a category with 200,000 monthly AI queries puts anywhere from roughly $43,000 to $1.5 million a year on the line, depending on how visible you are, the same demand, answered by you or by someone else.

One honest caveat, because a number that overclaims is worse than no number. AER measures revenue at stake, not revenue guaranteed. It answers one question: if the answers AI already gives converted the way search does, what is riding on them? Treat it as a leading indicator to track, not a forecast to bank.

That reframes the whole conversation. AI visibility stops being a marketing curiosity and becomes a line on the P&L, one your competitors are quietly collecting while you cannot see it. The first step is simply to measure it, so the cost stops being invisible.
